# Insight method — how we decide what to say

The single epistemology for *both* live matches and the Canon. **Data is the
arbiter, always.**

## The loop
1. **Analyse the data** (bottom-up) — the patterns the numbers show.
2. **Read the narrative** (top-down) — analyst commentary + the match transcript
   (minute-by-minute), when available.
3. **Surface candidate insights** from either side:
   - top-down **consensus** (multiple voices saying the same thing), or
   - something that **jumps out** in the data/transcript (an extreme, an outlier).
4. **Cross-check every candidate against the data.** The outcome decides the story:
   - **Confirmed** — narrative + data agree → strongest, most defensible insight.
   - **Myth** — narrative says X, data says no → the contrarian piece (lead with it).
   - **Discovery** — data shows what nobody mentioned → original insight.

A consensus the data can't back is **never kept as fact** — it's a lead, or a myth
to bust. (Same four-phase discipline as `canon.md`, applied live.)

## Reference-scope rule (avoids false assumptions from data gaps)
"Unusual" needs a yardstick that is actually **continuous/representative for that
metric** — or we invent norms from holes.

| Claim type | Valid reference |
|---|---|
| **Coarse** (goals, results, scoring rate) | the **continuous 1930+ Canon** |
| **Fine** (possession, xG, pressing, passmaps) | the **opponent** (this match) · the **team's recent form** · the **competition cohort** (e.g. all WC 2026 sides) |

- **No continuous fine-grained history exists** (not recorded pre-~2010s; tracking
  is 2022+) and it can't be bought — so we **never extrapolate a fine "historical
  norm."**
- **Tag every "unusual" claim with its reference** ("low *for a favourite in this
  tournament*," not "low historically").
- The fine reference **grows forward**: each WC 2026 match (and each new edition)
  deepens a complete cohort.

## Attribution (the firewall)
- **Own** the data-derived takes ("the numbers say…").
- **Attribute** the narrative takes ("analysts felt…", "ge.globo noted…").
- Patterns come from numbers; events and quotes come from the record.
