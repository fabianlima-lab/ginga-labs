# Ginga Labs — Brief (for design)

*Design freely from this. It tells you who we are, what we make, and why —
not how it should look. Surprise us.*

## Who we are
Ginga Labs is an AI that analyzes football — and is honest about it. Our motto is
**"soul & science":** we read the game in the numbers and tell it through the
players. Everything we publish is data-first, sourced like a journalist, and
openly admits what it doesn't know. We're an experiment, in public, in whether a
machine can truly *understand* the sport — not compute it like an outsider, but
feel it like the street. Brazilian-rooted, global-facing, bilingual (English +
Portuguese).

## What we do
We turn football data into writing with soul:
- **The Canon** — football history and tactical eras, mined from the data *first*,
  then checked against the legend — and we say plainly where the legend is a myth.
- **Live coverage** — analysis around matches as they happen (starting with the
  World Cup): the build-up, the post-game story, the standout player, the ratings,
  and what a whole round reveals.

Every piece is built the same disciplined way and tells you where it's sure and
where it's guessing.

## Why we do it
Two reasons, honestly: to find out whether AI can have a *real* role in sport, and
for the love of the game — to learn football more deeply and put cool, true things
into the world. It isn't a business. **The honesty is the product.**

## Who it's for
Football obsessives — people who care about tactics, history, and the numbers
behind the game. They read on their phones and arrive from social, so our work has
to grab inside a thread and stand on its own as a single shared image.

## Our pages
- **Home** — the front door: who we are at a glance, and a feed of everything we've
  published, easy to skim and pulling you into a piece.
- **A piece** — one read: a history deep-dive or a match story, mixing punchy text
  with visuals.
- **The visuals we make** (our share-images): data charts, team-shape / formation
  graphics, player cards, ratings — and, for historical deep-dives, full pass maps.

## One true constraint (not a style choice)
We never show data we don't have. A full pass network needs pass-by-pass data we
only hold for **historical** matches, so those visuals live in the Canon/deep-dive
pieces; for **live** games the honest visuals are team shape, stat comparisons, and
player cards. Whatever the design, it must be buildable from data we actually hold,
work in two languages, and live on a simple static site.

## Over to you
Give it an identity and bring the pages to life — type, color, layout, motion, the
lot. Make it feel like a real publication with a point of view, not a template.
