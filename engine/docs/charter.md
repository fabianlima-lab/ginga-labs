# Charter — what this project is (and isn't)

The north star. Every design decision answers to this.

## Why we're building it

A personal exploration: **does AI have a real role in sports?** Answered by
building the thing and seeing — can one person build a **complete, end-to-end
autonomous AI loop** that consumes real data, reasons about soccer, learns, and
puts cool content into the world?

The payoff is **learning and fun**, not a business.

## Goals

- **Build the full loop, end to end**: data → beliefs → content → post → learn,
  running **autonomously**. Watching it run itself is the point.
- **Leverage existing sources.** Consume what's already out there.
- **Learn soccer** more deeply by making the system reason about it.
- **Post cool content** online (under the Ginga Labs brand).
- **Have fun.**

## Non-goals (explicit)

- ❌ Compete with FIFA, Opta/StatsBomb, or any provider.
- ❌ Build our own intelligence layer — no DIY tracking / computer vision /
  data manufacturing. We *use* data; we don't *make* it.
- ❌ Monetize. Not required. If something fun happens to make money, fine — but
  it never drives a decision.
- ❌ Scale / market share / growth metrics.

## Principles

1. **Leverage, don't rebuild.** If a source exists, consume it; if it doesn't,
   we skip that depth — we don't go build it.
2. **Honesty makes it real.** The belief discipline (falsifiable, pre-registered)
   isn't for credibility-as-marketing — it's what makes the *learning* genuine
   and the content worth reading.
3. **Autonomous, with you as override.** The fun is the loop running itself.
4. **Small and fun beats big and grindy.** Prefer the version that's enjoyable
   to build and shows something working.

## What success looks like

The loop completes a full cycle on a real match **on its own**; you learn
something about soccer you didn't know; and a piece of content goes out that
*you* think is genuinely cool. That's it.
