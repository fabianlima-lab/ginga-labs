# The Defensive Era and the 1990 Nadir

- **id:** defensive-era-1990 · **grade:** coarse (scoring/results data; tactics via narrative)
- **built:** data-first, Phases 1–4 (the first holistic Canon entry)

## Two names tell the whole era
In **1958, Just Fontaine scored 13 goals** in a single World Cup — a record that
still stands. In **1990, the Golden Boot was won with 6**, by **Salvatore
Schillaci**, a Sicilian who had played *one* match for Italy before the tournament
began. Same prize, less than half the goals, won by a man nobody had picked. The
game had changed underneath everyone's feet.

And the team that reached the **1990 final scored five goals in the entire
tournament** — Argentina, the first side ever to win *two* penalty shootouts to
advance, three of their best players injured out, Maradona dragging a swollen
ankle from round to round. Five goals, all the way to Rome. That is the defensive
era in one stat line: **not conceding could carry you to a World Cup final.**

## The causal chain
- **Context** — by the 1970s football had got fit, organized and tactically
  literate; **two points for a win** made a draw a result worth keeping; and the
  keeper could still scoop a back-pass into his hands and kill the clock. Caution
  was *rational*.
- **System** — deep blocks, the catenaccio inheritance, and knockout sides that
  played 120 minutes for the shootout rather than risk the first goal.
- **Profile** — a sweeper behind, defenders who didn't lose their man, a lone
  target up top. The emblematic hero wasn't a playmaker; it was Schillaci, a
  grinder with wide eyes and a knack for the loose ball.
- **Data signature** — **2.21 goals/game in 1990**, a record low that *still*
  stands; the **Golden Boot won with exactly 6 for six straight tournaments
  (1978–98)**; finals settled 1-0 or on penalties.
- **Edge** — *not conceding.* Argentina proved it: five goals, two shootouts, a
  final.

## Phase 2 — data themes (blind, from our mining)
- goals/final: 5.0 (1930–70) → 4.0 (1974–86) → **1.4 (1990–2014)** → 6.0 (2018–22)
- top scorer: **13 (Fontaine '58)** … then **6, flat, 1978–98** … → 6.3 (2002–22)
- goals/match: **5.38 (1954, highest ever) → 2.21 (1990, lowest ever)**

Every signal points the same way: a high-scoring 1950s, a long slide to a floor
around 1990.

## Phase 3 — received wisdom (sourced)
- Italia '90 is remembered as one of the poorest World Cups, sides playing for
  penalties; **Schillaci** the surprise top scorer with one prior cap;
  **Argentina** a record-low five goals for a finalist. [Wikipedia — *1990 FIFA
  World Cup*]
- It directly prompted the **back-pass rule (1992)** and **three points for a win
  (WC 1994)**, explicitly to make negative football less viable; the back-pass ban
  is now called one of the game's most successful rule changes — and it launched
  the **sweeper-keeper / playing out from the back**. [Wikipedia — *Back-pass rule*]

## Phase 4 — reconciliation
- **CONFIRMED.** The data-defined nadir *is* the narrative's nadir. 1990 is the
  floor in every signal, and the books agree it was the low point. The peak→slide
  arc is real, and the nadir→rule-change story checks out (1992, 1994).
- **DISCOVERY / TENSION.** The narrative says those rule changes "revived attacking
  football." Our data **doesn't show an immediate revival** — finals stayed tight
  (1-0s and shootouts) and the Golden Boot sat at ~6 for two more decades; the
  reversal only appears in **2018/2022, a quarter-century later**. So the "rule
  changes fixed the scoring" story is at least oversimplified. *Caveat:* we hold
  finals + top-scorer, not the full goals/game series — the ban may have lifted
  overall goals even as the knockouts stayed cagey. **Flagged, not closed.**
- **Where the ban actually landed.** Its real legacy isn't goals — it's the
  **sweeper-keeper → playing out from the back → the modern control era** our
  2018–2022 data shows *winning*. It didn't add goals; it rewired build-up.

## Relations
- **Ended by:** rule changes (1992/94) on the incentive side; later, the
  possession/pressing evolution on the tactical side.
- **Lineage:** back-pass ban → sweeper-keeper → playing out from the back → the
  control era (cf. 2018–2022: control ≈ success).

## Register note
A *coarse* entry — it dates and frames the era from results and the people who
embodied it. The deeper *why* needs narrative plus finer (event) data we don't
have before 2018.
