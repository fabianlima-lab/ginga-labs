# The Defensive Era and the 1990 Nadir

- **id:** defensive-era-1990 · **grade:** coarse (scoring/results data; tactics via narrative)
- **built:** data-first, Phases 1–4 (the first holistic Canon entry)

## The causal chain
- **Context** — by the 1970s the game had got fit, organized, and tactically
  literate; **two points for a win** made a draw a respectable result; and the
  keeper could still scoop up a back-pass and run down the clock. Low-risk
  football was *rational*.
- **System** — deep, disciplined blocks, the catenaccio inheritance, and in the
  knockouts teams that "played 120 minutes for the shootout" rather than risk a
  goal.
- **Profile** — a libero to sweep, defenders who didn't lose their man, a lone
  target up top. You didn't need flair; you needed to not concede.
- **Data signature** — scoring hit a floor: **2.21 goals/game in 1990** (a record
  low that still stands), the **Golden Boot stuck on exactly 6 for seven straight
  tournaments (1978–98)**, and finals decided by a single goal or on penalties.
- **Edge** — *not conceding.* With a draw worth keeping and the clock easy to
  kill, the side that refused to lose often went furthest.

## Phase 2 — data themes (blind, from our mining)
- goals/final: 5.0 (1930–70) → 4.0 (1974–86) → **1.4 (1990–2014)** → 6.0 (2018–22)
- top scorer: 8.8 (1930–58, Fontaine **13**) → **6.0 flat (1978–98)** → 6.3
- goals/match: **5.38 (1954, highest) → 2.21 (1990, lowest)**

Every signal: peak in the 50s, nadir around 1990.

## Phase 3 — received wisdom (sourced)
- Italia '90 is "one of the poorest World Cups," sides playing for penalties;
  2.21 goals/game a record low. [Wikipedia — *1990 FIFA World Cup*]
- It directly prompted the **back-pass rule (1992)** and **three points for a win
  (WC 1994)**, explicitly to make negative football less viable. The back-pass ban
  is now called one of the most successful rule changes — and it launched the
  **sweeper-keeper / playing out from the back**. [Wikipedia — *Back-pass rule*]

## Phase 4 — reconciliation
- **CONFIRMED.** The data-defined nadir *is* the narrative's nadir — 1990 is the
  floor in every signal we have, and the books agree it was the defensive low
  point. The peak→contraction arc is real.
- **CONFIRMED (factual).** Nadir → rule-change causality checks out: 1992 back-pass
  ban, 1994 three points, both post-1990, both aimed at the negativity.
- **DISCOVERY / TENSION.** The narrative says the rule changes "revived attacking
  football." Our World Cup data **doesn't show an immediate revival** — finals
  stayed tight (1-0s and shootouts) and the Golden Boot stayed ~6 for two more
  decades; the finals reversal only shows up in **2018/2022, a quarter-century
  later**. So at the World Cup level, "the rule changes fixed the scoring" is at
  least oversimplified.
  *Caveat:* we have finals + top-scorer, not the full tournament-wide goals/game
  series (the proper test). The ban may have lifted overall goals/game even as the
  knockouts stayed cagey. **Flagged, not closed.**
- **Where the ban actually landed.** Its biggest legacy in our data's direction
  isn't goals — it's the **sweeper-keeper and playing out from the back**, the
  seed of the possession/control football our fine 2018–2022 data shows *winning*.
  The rule didn't obviously add goals; it rewired build-up.

## Relations
- **Ended by:** rule changes (1992/94) on the incentive side; later, the
  possession/pressing evolution on the tactical side.
- **Lineage:** back-pass ban → sweeper-keeper → playing out from the back → the
  modern **control era** (cf. our 2018–2022 holistic finding: control ≈ success).
